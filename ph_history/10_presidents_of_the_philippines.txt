PRESIDENTS OF THE PHILIPPINES
==============================

THE FIRST PHILIPPINE REPUBLIC

Emilio Aguinaldo (President, 1899-1901)
Aguinaldo was the first president of the Philippines, heading the First Philippine Republic inaugurated at Malolos, Bulacan on January 23, 1899. His government issued the Malolos Constitution, the first democratic constitution in Asia. His presidency ended when he was captured by American forces in 1901 and swore allegiance to the United States. He lived until 1964, dying at the age of 94, and remained a controversial figure — admired as a revolutionary leader, criticized for the execution of Andres Bonifacio and for his cooperation with Japanese occupation forces during World War II.

THE COMMONWEALTH PERIOD (1935-1946)

Manuel L. Quezon (President, 1935-1944)
Quezon was the dominant Filipino politician of the early 20th century. As Senate President, he had already wielded enormous political power before being elected the first President of the Commonwealth of the Philippines in 1935. He made Tagalog the basis of the national language and worked to build Philippine institutions ahead of independence. When the Japanese invaded in 1941, he evacuated to the United States and led a government-in-exile. He died of tuberculosis in Saranac Lake, New York, in August 1944, before seeing independence.

Sergio Osmena (President, 1944-1946)
Osmena succeeded Quezon as president upon Quezon's death. He returned to the Philippines with MacArthur in 1944 and oversaw the country through the devastation of liberation. He lost the 1946 election to Manuel Roxas.

THE THIRD REPUBLIC (1946-1972)

Manuel Roxas (President, 1946-1948)
Roxas was the first president of the independent Third Republic of the Philippines, inaugurated on July 4, 1946. He had served under the Japanese occupation government, which some held against him, but American authorities cleared him of collaboration charges. He died in office in April 1948 of a heart attack.

Elpidio Quirino (President, 1948-1953)
Quirino succeeded Roxas and won the 1949 election in his own right. His administration was marked by the Hukbalahap communist insurgency, which he struggled to contain. He was defeated in 1953 by Ramon Magsaysay.

Ramon Magsaysay (President, 1953-1957)
Magsaysay is remembered as one of the most beloved Philippine presidents. As Secretary of National Defense before his presidency, he effectively dismantled the Hukbalahap rebellion through a combination of military pressure and genuine reforms for rural Filipinos. As president, he was known for his direct accessibility to ordinary people, reportedly opening Malacañang Palace to the public. He died in a plane crash on Mount Manunggal in Cebu on March 17, 1957.

Carlos P. Garcia (President, 1957-1961)
Garcia promoted the Filipino First Policy, prioritizing Filipino ownership and participation in the national economy over foreign (particularly American) business interests. He introduced the austerity program to address foreign exchange shortages.

Diosdado Macapagal (President, 1961-1965)
Macapagal moved the celebration of Philippine Independence Day from July 4 (the date of American recognition in 1946) to June 12 (the date of Aguinaldo's 1898 declaration). He also pursued agrarian reform legislation. He was defeated by Ferdinand Marcos in 1965. His daughter, Gloria Macapagal Arroyo, later became the 14th president.

Ferdinand Marcos (President, 1965-1986)
Marcos won the presidency in 1965 and was re-elected in 1969. He declared Martial Law in 1972, suspended the constitution, and ruled as a dictator for 14 years. He was ousted by the People Power Revolution in February 1986 and died in exile in Hawaii in 1989. (See: Ferdinand Marcos and Martial Law)

THE RESTORATION OF DEMOCRACY (1986-PRESENT)

Corazon Aquino (President, 1986-1992)
Corazon "Cory" Aquino became the 11th President of the Philippines and the first woman to hold the office after the People Power Revolution. She restored democratic institutions: released political prisoners, allowed a new constitution to be drafted and ratified in 1987, and held free elections. Her presidency survived seven coup attempts by military factions unhappy with the democratic transition. She chose not to seek re-election.

Fidel Ramos (President, 1992-1998)
Ramos, one of the key military figures of the People Power Revolution, won the 1992 election with less than 24 percent of the vote in a crowded field. His presidency is associated with economic growth and a peace process with Muslim separatist groups in Mindanao. He brought the Philippines into ASEAN's economic integration and presided over a period of relative stability.

Joseph Estrada (President, 1998-2001)
A former movie actor, Estrada won the 1998 election in a landslide on a pro-poor campaign. He became the first Philippine president to be impeached by the House of Representatives, on charges of plunder. His impeachment trial collapsed when senators voted to block the opening of key evidence. Millions of Filipinos took to EDSA in a second People Power protest (EDSA II), and the Supreme Court declared the presidency vacant. Estrada was replaced by his vice president.

Gloria Macapagal Arroyo (President, 2001-2010)
Arroyo succeeded Estrada and later won the 2004 election, though the results were disputed amid allegations of fraud captured in a recorded conversation between Arroyo and an election official. Her presidency saw significant economic growth but was plagued by political scandals. She was later arrested and charged with electoral sabotage, but was eventually acquitted.

Benigno Aquino III (President, 2010-2016)
Son of Corazon and Benigno Aquino Jr., the younger Aquino won in 2010 on an anti-corruption platform. His presidency saw robust economic growth and a landmark peace agreement with the Moro Islamic Liberation Front in Mindanao. He filed the international arbitration case against China over the South China Sea, which the Philippines won in 2016.

Rodrigo Duterte (President, 2016-2022)
Duterte won the 2016 election as a political outsider known for his blunt language and record as mayor of Davao City. His presidency was defined by a brutal anti-drug campaign that resulted in thousands of extrajudicial killings — estimates range from 6,000 (official police data) to over 27,000 (human rights organizations). He also pivoted toward China and Russia and away from the United States.

Ferdinand Marcos Jr. (President, 2022-present)
The son of the late dictator was elected president in May 2022 by a wide margin, with Sara Duterte (daughter of Rodrigo Duterte) as his vice president. His election, coming 36 years after his father was ousted, was seen by supporters as a mandate for national unity and by critics as historical revisionism and the normalization of the Marcos family's legacy.
