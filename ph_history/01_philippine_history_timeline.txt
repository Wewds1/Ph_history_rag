PHILIPPINE HISTORY: KEY EVENTS TIMELINE
========================================

PRE-COLONIAL PERIOD (Before 1565)

- c. 709,000 BCE: Earliest evidence of hominin activity in the Philippine archipelago.
- c. 134,000 BCE: Homo luzonensis, an archaic human species, lived on the island of Luzon.
- c. 47,000 BCE: Tabon Man, the earliest known anatomically modern human in the Philippines, lived in Tabon Caves, Palawan.
- c. 3000 BCE: Austronesian peoples migrated southward from Taiwan, forming the majority of the current Filipino population.
- c. 900 CE: The Laguna Copperplate Inscription is produced, the oldest known written document found in the Philippines.
- 1300s: Chinese trade with the Philippine archipelago is well established. The Sulu Sultanate and other coastal polities traded actively with China and neighboring kingdoms.
- 1380: Muslim Arab missionaries arrive at the Sulu Archipelago, beginning the spread of Islam in the southern Philippines.
- 1400s-1500s: The Sultanate of Brunei expands its influence over coastal Luzon and Mindanao through trade and political marriages.

SPANISH COLONIAL PERIOD (1565-1898)

- 1521: Ferdinand Magellan arrives in the Philippines, claims the islands for Spain, and is killed in the Battle of Mactan by Datu Lapulapu.
- 1565: Miguel Lopez de Legazpi establishes the first permanent Spanish settlement in Cebu.
- 1571: Legazpi founds Manila as the colonial capital, defeating the Muslim rulers of the area.
- 1593: Fray Juan de Plasencia writes the Doctrina Christiana, one of the earliest printed books in the Philippines.
- 1611: The University of Santo Tomas (UST) is founded in Manila, making it the oldest university in Asia.
- 1762-1764: British forces briefly occupy Manila during the Seven Years' War.
- 1781: The Philippine tobacco monopoly is established, forcing farmers to grow tobacco for the colonial government.
- 1872: The Cavite Mutiny occurs. Three Filipino priests — Gomez, Burgos, and Zamora (GomBurZa) — are executed on charges of sedition, sparking nationalist sentiment.
- 1872-1892: The Propaganda Movement, led by Jose Rizal, Marcelo del Pilar, and Graciano Lopez Jaena, advocates for reforms through writing and publication in Europe.
- 1887: Jose Rizal publishes Noli Me Tangere, exposing the abuses of the Spanish friars and colonial government.
- 1891: Rizal publishes El Filibusterismo, the sequel to Noli Me Tangere.
- 1892: Rizal returns to the Philippines and founds La Liga Filipina, a civic organization. He is arrested days later and exiled to Dapitan, Mindanao.
- 1892: Andres Bonifacio founds the Katipunan (KKK), a secret revolutionary society dedicated to independence through armed revolt. Membership eventually reaches an estimated 100,000.
- August 23, 1896: The Cry of Pugad Lawin (or Balintawak) — Bonifacio and his Katipuneros tear their cedulas (residence certificates) and call for open revolution against Spain.
- December 30, 1896: Jose Rizal is executed by firing squad at Bagumbayan (now Rizal Park), Manila. He is 35 years old.
- 1897: Emilio Aguinaldo takes command of the revolution after the Tejeros Convention. Bonifacio is later arrested and executed on Aguinaldo's orders.
- December 1897: The Pact of Biak-na-Bato temporarily ends the conflict. Aguinaldo and other leaders go into exile in Hong Kong after receiving a cash payment.
- 1898: The Spanish-American War begins. The US Navy under Admiral George Dewey defeats the Spanish fleet at the Battle of Manila Bay on May 1.
- June 12, 1898: Emilio Aguinaldo proclaims Philippine independence at Kawit, Cavite. The Philippine flag is unfurled for the first time.
- January 23, 1899: The First Philippine Republic is inaugurated in Malolos, Bulacan, with Aguinaldo as president.
- December 10, 1898: Spain cedes the Philippines to the United States in the Treaty of Paris for $20 million.

AMERICAN COLONIAL PERIOD (1898-1946)

- February 4, 1899: The Philippine-American War begins when American soldiers fire on Filipino soldiers near Manila.
- 1901: Aguinaldo is captured and pledges allegiance to the United States. Organized resistance continues for years, particularly in Mindanao and Samar.
- 1902: The Philippine-American War is officially declared over by the United States, though fighting continues.
- 1907: The First Philippine Assembly convenes, giving Filipinos limited self-governance.
- 1916: The Jones Act grants the Philippines greater autonomy and promises independence once a stable government is established.
- 1927: Filipino teachers make up nearly all of the 26,200 teachers in public schools, a rapid transformation from the all-American teacher corps of 1901.
- 1935: The Philippine Commonwealth is established. Manuel L. Quezon is elected its first president.
- December 8, 1941: Japan attacks the Philippines one day after Pearl Harbor. Bataan falls in April 1942 after months of brutal fighting.
- 1942: The Bataan Death March — American and Filipino prisoners of war are forced to march over 60 miles under brutal conditions, resulting in thousands of deaths.
- 1944-1945: American and Filipino forces liberate the Philippines. The Battle of Manila in 1945 destroys much of the city and kills an estimated 100,000 civilians.
- July 4, 1946: The United States grants independence to the Republic of the Philippines. Manuel Roxas is elected the first president of the independent republic.

POST-INDEPENDENCE PERIOD (1946-present)

- 1953: Ramon Magsaysay is elected president. He is credited with defeating the communist Huk rebellion and is remembered for his closeness to ordinary Filipinos.
- 1965: Ferdinand Marcos is elected president.
- September 21, 1972: Marcos declares Martial Law, citing communist rebellion and civil disorder. He suspends the constitution, shuts down Congress, and arrests political opponents.
- August 21, 1983: Benigno "Ninoy" Aquino Jr. is assassinated upon returning from exile in the United States, shot on the tarmac of Manila International Airport.
- February 7, 1986: Snap presidential elections are held. Marcos is declared the winner over Corazon Aquino amid widespread allegations of fraud.
- February 22-25, 1986: The People Power Revolution (EDSA Revolution). Over two million Filipinos gather on Epifanio de los Santos Avenue in Manila. Marcos and his family flee to Hawaii. Corazon Aquino is inaugurated as the 11th President of the Philippines.
- 1991: Mount Pinatubo erupts on June 15, one of the largest volcanic eruptions of the 20th century. The eruption devastates surrounding provinces.
- 1998: Joseph Estrada is elected president on a pro-poor platform.
- 2001: Estrada is removed from office through a second People Power movement (EDSA II) and is replaced by Vice President Gloria Macapagal Arroyo.
- 2013: Typhoon Haiyan (Yolanda), one of the strongest tropical cyclones ever recorded, strikes the Visayas on November 8, killing over 6,000 people.
- 2016: Rodrigo Duterte is elected president on an anti-drug platform.
- 2016: The Permanent Court of Arbitration rules in favor of the Philippines in its case against China over territorial disputes in the South China Sea.
- 2022: Ferdinand Marcos Jr., son of the former dictator, is elected president of the Philippines.
