PHILIPPINE GEOGRAPHY AND REGIONS
==================================

THE ARCHIPELAGO

The Philippines is an archipelago of 7,641 islands located in Southeast Asia, in the western Pacific Ocean. It lies between latitudes 4 and 21 degrees north, with the Philippine Sea to the east, the South China Sea to the west, and the Celebes Sea to the south. The total land area is approximately 300,000 square kilometers.

The islands are grouped into three major island groups: Luzon in the north, the Visayas in the center, and Mindanao in the south. Luzon is the largest island and contains Manila, the national capital. Mindanao is the second largest. The Visayas consist of several mid-sized islands including Cebu, Bohol, Leyte, Samar, Negros, and Panay.

LUZON

Luzon is the most populous and economically dominant island. It contains Metro Manila — the National Capital Region — which houses over 13 million people in the city proper and over 24 million in the greater urban agglomeration, making it one of the largest and most densely populated urban areas in the world.

Key regions and cities in Luzon include:
- Metro Manila (National Capital Region): the political, economic, and cultural center of the country. Contains Manila, Quezon City, Makati, and 13 other cities and municipalities.
- Ilocos Region (Ilocos Norte and Ilocos Sur): the ancestral homeland of the Ilocano people. Vigan, a UNESCO World Heritage city, preserves Spanish colonial architecture.
- Cagayan Valley: the Cagayan River, the longest in the Philippines, runs through this region. The valley is rich in archaeological sites yielding evidence of early human habitation.
- Central Luzon: includes Pampanga, Tarlac, and Bulacan — provinces with deep historical significance. Clark Freeport Zone, site of the former Clark Air Base, is here.
- CALABARZON (Cavite, Laguna, Batangas, Rizal, Quezon): the most industrialized region outside Metro Manila. Cavite has particular historical significance as the birthplace of Emilio Aguinaldo and site of the 1898 Declaration of Independence.
- Cordillera Administrative Region: the highland region of northern Luzon, homeland of the Igorot peoples. The Banaue Rice Terraces, carved into the Ifugao mountainside over 2,000 years ago, are often called the Eighth Wonder of the World.
- Bicol Region: the southeastern peninsula of Luzon, known for the Mayon Volcano — considered the world's most perfectly shaped volcanic cone.

THE VISAYAS

The Visayas are the central islands, home to the Visayan language group — the most widely spoken language family in the Philippines by number of speakers, surpassing Tagalog.

Key areas include:
- Cebu: the oldest city in the Philippines, founded by Legazpi in 1565. Cebu City is the regional economic hub and a major port. Cebu is considered the birthplace of Philippine Christianity — the Santo Nino de Cebu, a statue brought by Magellan in 1521, is the oldest Christian relic in the Philippines.
- Bohol: known for the Chocolate Hills — a geological formation of over 1,200 symmetrical hills that turn brown in the dry season. Also home to the Philippine tarsier, one of the world's smallest primates.
- Leyte and Samar: scene of some of the fiercest fighting of World War II. MacArthur waded ashore at Leyte Gulf in 1944. Samar was the site of the Balangiga Massacre and subsequent American reprisals.
- Negros: historically dominated by sugar plantations and haciendas, Negros was the center of the Philippine sugar industry.
- Iloilo (Panay): a historic trade center and one of the country's most livable cities.

MINDANAO

Mindanao is the second-largest island and the most culturally diverse. It is home to the majority of Philippine Muslims (Moros), centered in the Bangsamoro region in the west and south.

Key areas include:
- Davao City: the largest city in Mindanao and one of the largest in the Philippines by area. Rodrigo Duterte served as mayor of Davao for over two decades before becoming president.
- Zamboanga City: a coastal city at the western tip of Mindanao with a distinct culture and language (Chavacano, a Spanish-based creole).
- Bangsamoro Autonomous Region in Muslim Mindanao (BARMM): established in 2019 following a peace agreement with the Moro Islamic Liberation Front, replacing the older ARMM. The region has its own parliament and expanded autonomy.
- Mount Apo: at 2,954 meters, this is the highest peak in the Philippines. Located in the Davao del Sur and North Cotabato provinces.

PALAWAN

Palawan is a long, narrow island stretching from Mindoro to Borneo. It is sometimes called the "Last Frontier" for its biodiversity and relatively undeveloped natural environment. The Palawan Underground River, Puerto Princesa, is a UNESCO World Heritage Site and one of the New Seven Wonders of Nature.

GEOLOGY AND NATURAL HAZARDS

The Philippines sits on the Pacific Ring of Fire and is one of the most geologically active countries on earth. It has about 300 volcanoes, of which 24 are active. The most notable volcanic events in modern history include:

- The 1991 eruption of Mount Pinatubo in Zambales, one of the largest volcanic eruptions of the 20th century. The eruption killed over 800 people and displaced over 200,000. The ash and sulfur dioxide injected into the stratosphere cooled global temperatures by about 0.5 degrees Celsius for two years.
- Mayon Volcano in Albay has erupted over 50 times since 1616 and remains one of the most active volcanoes in the Philippines.
- Taal Volcano, located in a lake south of Manila, had a significant eruption in January 2020.

The Philippines experiences an average of 20 typhoons per year. The strongest typhoon ever recorded at landfall was Typhoon Haiyan (Yolanda) on November 8, 2013, which struck the Eastern Visayas with winds exceeding 315 kilometers per hour, killing over 6,000 people and displacing 4 million.

The country also sits along several active fault lines. Destructive earthquakes have struck throughout Philippine history, including a 7.7-magnitude earthquake in 1990 that devastated Baguio City and the northern Luzon region.
